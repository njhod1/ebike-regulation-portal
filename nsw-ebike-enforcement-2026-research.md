# NSW E-Bike & Cycling Enforcement — 2026 Research Summary

Source: Claude.ai chat, August 2026. Compiled from web search results.

## Context / Trigger
Observation from a bus between Mona Vale and Narrabeen (Northern Beaches, NSW): 7–8 helmetless bike riders and 2 apparently illegal e-bikes in a single trip, with no visible enforcement.

## Reporting Options (NSW)
- **Police Assistance Line — 131 444**: non-urgent reports of traffic offences, including no-helmet riding and illegal e-bikes/e-scooters. Can supply location, time, description, photos/video.
- **Crime Stoppers — 1800 333 000**: anonymous, though pitched at more serious crime than helmet compliance.
- **Transport for NSW / local council**: some councils run e-bike/e-scooter compliance campaigns and accept reports on illegal devices.
- Reporting a *specific location/time pattern* (e.g. "weekday mornings ~8am, Mona Vale–Narrabeen corridor") is more actionable for police/council tasking than a one-off sighting.

## Northern Beaches Council — "Know the Code" Campaign
- Council resolution passed September 2023; campaign rolled out 2024–2025.
- Billed as Australia's first behavioural-change campaign specifically for e-bike safety.
- Three-point code (mirrors the "surfer's code"), aimed at ~14-year-old riders and parents:
  1. Slow down to walking pace when others are on the path
  2. Ring your bell and call "on your right"
  3. Be ready for sudden changes (people, pets, prams)
- Delivered via signage (60 locations), school posters/newsletters, social media, partnerships with Bicycle NSW and local police.
- Council-reported outcome: reduction in complaints raised to Council.
- **Key limitation, stated explicitly by Council**: councils are *not* the regulating authority for e-bikes — education only, no enforcement power. Council has lobbied NSW Government for years for actual enforcement reform.

## 2026 State-Level Enforcement Rollout

### New legislation
- **Road Transport Amendment (Non-registrable Motor Vehicles) Bill 2026** passed NSW Parliament.
- Gives NSW Police and Transport Authorised Officers power to **seize and crush** e-bikes that effectively perform as motorbikes.
- Source: https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/e-bike-seizure-and-crushing-powers-to-become-law
- Related ministerial release: https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/a-crushing-outcome-for-owners-and-riders-of-illegal-e-bikes

### Tightened technical standard
- Legal e-bikes capped at **250W continuous power** (down from 500W tolerance).
- Motor assistance must **cut out at 25 km/h**.
- **Throttle-only propulsion banned above 6 km/h** (walking pace) — above that, motor may only assist while pedalling.
- Alignment with European standard **EN 15194**; three-year transition period 2026–2029 for consumers/retailers.
- Source: https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/bikes-e-bikes-e-scooters/bicycles-electric-bikes/e-bike-faqs

### Roadside dyno (dynamometer) testing
- Portable dyno units imported from Europe/UK (already proven technology there).
- $100,000 funded from the Community Road Safety Fund for **3 units**, trialled by NSW Police and Transport for NSW in known illegal e-bike hotspots.
- Criticised by some (incl. NRMA) as too few units for the scale of the problem.
- Source: https://www.mynrma.com.au/open-road/news/2026/tighter-laws-for-e-bikes

### Comparable precedent — WA
- **Operation Moorhead** (Perth, early January 2026): police seized 36 e-rideables, charged 25 juveniles (aged 11–16) and 4 adults with traffic offences including "no authority to drive" and "use of unlicensed vehicle on a road."
- NSW crackdown explicitly modelled on this approach.
- Source: https://bicyclenetwork.com.au/newsroom/2026/02/12/nsw-police-to-seize-and-crush-illegal-e-bikes/

### Local council enforcement blitz example (Tweed Shire)
- Paired approach: 1 week education (pop-up stalls, NSW Police + Transport for NSW collaboration) → 1 week active enforcement.
- Fines:
  - Footpath riding (over 16, not accompanying a child): $136
  - Failing to wear a helmet: $410
  - Illegal e-bike modifications: from $723
- Source: https://www.tweed.nsw.gov.au/council/news-updates/latest-news/general-news/ebike-blitz

## Structural Reasons Enforcement Has Lagged
- Councils have **no jurisdiction** to enforce e-bike/cycling road rules — sits entirely with NSW Police. Confirmed by Sutherland Shire Council's submission to the NSW Parliamentary Inquiry, which also noted no current political appetite to transfer these powers to councils.
- Modified/non-compliant e-bikes are **genuinely hard to identify roadside** without dyno equipment — visual inspection alone is unreliable, especially with minors involved.
- General resourcing competition: cycling/e-bike compliance competes with other policing priorities.
- Source: https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/lcdocs/submissions/87354/0121%20-%20Sutherland%20Shire%20Council.pdf

## Bottom Line
- **Helmet non-compliance** is the more immediately actionable offence — flat $410 fine, no special equipment needed, just an officer present.
- **Illegal e-bike enforcement** is improving rapidly through 2026 (crush powers, dyno trials, tightened technical standard) but is still early-stage/uneven — only 3 dyno units nationally as of this research.
- Reporting specific corridors/times (e.g. Mona Vale–Narrabeen) is worthwhile: it can feed into the "known hotspot" targeting these dyno trials are using.
